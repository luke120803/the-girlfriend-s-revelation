# Skill: content-writing

## Purpose
This skill defines how to write copy for intimate, emotional, personal web experiences.
Use it when writing preambles, letters, card messages, quiz questions, friend messages, or any text that must feel authored and specific — not generic or AI-generated.

## Core philosophy
Every word should feel like it was written by a person who knows the recipient.
If the text could appear on any birthday site, it is not good enough.
Specificity is the only thing that creates emotional resonance.

## Tone principles
- Write as if speaking directly to one person, not an audience.
- Prefer short, weighted sentences over long, flowing ones.
- Leave space for the reader to feel — do not over-explain.
- Avoid exclamation marks as a substitute for genuine emotion.
- Avoid filler words: "incrível", "especial", "único" without context are meaningless.
- Use the recipient's name sparingly — when used, it should land with weight.

## Voice for this project
- Intimate, not childish.
- Warm, not saccharine.
- Cinematic, not theatrical.
- Direct, not formal.
- Personal, not poetic for the sake of it.

## Writing by screen

### Cover (Tela 1)
- One short phrase in Boska italic — creates mystery, not celebration.
- The title in Clash Display — clear, direct, no flourish.
- The date as a quiet anchor, not a countdown.
- The CTA button: one or two words, imperative, calm.

### Preamble (Tela 2)
- 2–3 sentences maximum.
- Sets the emotional pace for what follows.
- Should feel like a breath before something meaningful.
- Avoid: "Preparei algo especial para você" — too generic.
- Prefer: something that acknowledges the moment without over-promising.

### Reveal cards (Tela 3)
- Each card has a short title and a short body (2–4 lines max).
- The title should be a fragment, not a full sentence.
- The body should feel like a memory or a specific observation, not a compliment.
- Avoid: "Você é incrível porque…"
- Prefer: a specific moment, detail, or truth about the person.

### Friend messages (Tela 4)
- Each friend's message should sound like that friend, not like a template.
- Keep them short: 2–5 lines.
- If the message is too long, edit it — do not display walls of text.
- The name attribution should be simple: just the name, no title or emoji.

### Letter (Tela 5)
- The most personal text in the experience.
- Written in first person, directly to Minnie.
- Should have a beginning, a middle, and an end — even if short.
- The typewriter effect means each line must work on its own as it appears.
- Avoid generic declarations. Prefer specific memories, observations, or promises.

### Closing (Tela 6)
- Short. One or two lines.
- Should feel like an exhale after the emotional peak.
- The share CTA should be warm but not pushy.

## Anti-patterns
Avoid:
- "Você é a pessoa mais especial da minha vida" without context
- Emoji as emotional punctuation
- Rhymes that feel forced
- Motivational quote energy
- Copy that could be copy-pasted to anyone
- Overuse of ellipsis (…) as a dramatic device
- Starting every sentence with "Você"

## Quiz copy guidance
- Questions should feel like a game between two people who know each other.
- Avoid trivia-style phrasing ("Em que ano…", "Qual foi o primeiro…").
- Prefer intimate framing ("O que você acha que eu mais gosto em…", "Se eu pudesse…").
- Answer options should have personality — not just A/B/C labels.
- Feedback after each answer should be warm and specific, not generic ("Correto!" is not enough).

## Output expectations
When applying this skill, the agent should:
- read the project context before writing anything
- write copy that is specific to Minnie, not generic
- propose alternatives when the first draft feels flat
- flag any text that sounds like it could appear on any birthday site
- prefer fewer, better words over more words
